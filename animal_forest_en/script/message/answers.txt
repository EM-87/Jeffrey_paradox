# Our English for questions the GameCube's English asks for answers of its own: where the GameCube's game
# offers other answers than the cartridge's (its choices are its message's own codes; the en build runs the
# cartridge's), its question can ask for one the cartridge's window does not give (how a tourney goes,
# answered with a plain yes), and its reply can answer one the player did not choose. From the cartridge's
# text, so that the question asks what the cartridge's answers answer, and the reply follows the answer:
#
# - true or false: the cartridge's quizzes are answered with a circle or a cross (answers 205 and 206,
#   which the shape games share): each question says which is which (2919-2929, 7313, 9322-9329);
# - answer 16, a polite no in English, is also "fine by me" on the cartridge: where it leads to yes, the
#   question is asked so that no reads as yes (6248, 7392, and 10176 in events.txt);
# - answer 183 is the pear in the fruit guess, and the cartridge's word for it also means "none": the
#   question offers pears as the way out (4974);
# - the eye test's second letter is the cartridge's third answer, M (10672);
# - a mealtime phrase said for a greeting (9702, 9707) is the cartridge's joke, which the answers play on;
# - the replies 4008, 4009 and 9725 answer the cartridge's answers.
#
# tools/msgbank.py's dump form, as ours.txt (its header says how the build lays it over the bank and what
# af_text.py holds it to).
